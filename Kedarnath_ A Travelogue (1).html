Kedarnath

Sixteen kilometres uphill, one cold river, and a temple at the edge of the snow line.

3,583 m · Rudraprayag, Uttarakhand

Why go to a place that closes for winter?

Jyotirlinga of ShivaMandakini valleyOpen roughly May to November

Kedarnath sits in a bowl of peaks above the Mandakini river, and for most of the year you can only reach it on foot, by pony, or by helicopter. The temple is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and a stop on the Char Dham circuit.

This is my account of the walk up: what it felt like, what I wish I had packed, and what stayed with me afterwards. Replace these notes with your own story as you go.

From the notebook

Day 1

Rishikesh to Sonprayag

A long day of switchbacks beside the Ganga, then the Mandakini. Rain on the windscreen near Rudraprayag, where two rivers meet in two colours. We reached Sonprayag after dark and slept early.

Day 2

The walk up

We left Gaurikund at five. Ponies, porters and chanting pilgrims shared the same narrow path. The last stretch felt slower than the rest, but then the temple appeared, small and dark against the snow, and everyone around me went quiet.

Day 3

Evening aarti and the walk back

The aarti at dusk was the highlight: bells, cold hands, breath turning to mist. In the morning we climbed a little higher to Bhairavnath temple for a view over the whole valley before heading down.

Before you go

Best time

May to June and September to October. Monsoon months bring landslides and closed roads.

Getting there

Road from Rishikesh or Haridwar to Sonprayag, then Gaurikund. Helicopter services also run from Phata, Sersi and Guptkashi.

What to pack

Layers, rain jacket, broken-in shoes, trekking poles, a torch, and a power bank. Nights are cold at any time of year.

Registration

Pilgrims need to register before the trek. Check the current rules on the official Uttarakhand tourism channels before you travel.
